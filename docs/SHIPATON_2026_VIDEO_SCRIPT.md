# Top Down RACE — Shipaton 2026 Presentation Video

## Purpose

This document is the master script for the Shipaton 2026 submission video for **Top Down RACE**, targeting the **Games** category.

The video is not a generic advertisement. Its job is to make the judges understand, within less than two minutes, what Top Down RACE is, why it feels different, how much real product is already there, and why it is worth downloading and testing.

**Target duration:** 1:45–1:55.

**Core rule:** the game itself must be the hero. Generated cinematic material can elevate the presentation, but it must never replace or misrepresent real gameplay.

---

# Creative thesis

**Top Down RACE is a mobile racing game built around driving feel, personality, progression and control — not just tapping left and right around a track.**

The video should communicate four things very quickly:

1. It looks distinctive.
2. It feels satisfying to drive.
3. It contains real depth beyond the race itself.
4. It is already a real playable product, not a concept trailer.

The emotional arc should be:

**Hook → Proof → Depth → Variety → Personality → Final punch.**

---

# Editing language

The presentation should combine three visual sources:

- **Real gameplay capture:** the backbone of the video.
- **Real UI/navigation capture:** proof of product depth and polish.
- **Flow-generated cinematic inserts:** short support shots for atmosphere, branding, transitions and emotional lift.

Generated shots must stay visually compatible with the game. Avoid any cinematic shot that implies mechanics, environments or graphical fidelity that the playable product does not contain.

Recommended pacing:

- 1–3 second cuts during racing montages.
- 2–4 second cuts for UI and progression.
- No long static menu shots.
- Avoid explaining systems with paragraphs of text.
- Let the image do most of the work.

Music should build continuously and reach its strongest point around 1:15–1:35, then leave room for the final logo/claim.

# MASTER SCRIPT — V1

## 0:00–0:05 — THE HOOK

### Goal

Make the judge stop thinking of this as "another small mobile racing game" before five seconds have passed.

### Image

Start directly on movement, not on a black screen.

Preferred opening:

- 1–2 second Flow cinematic insert inspired by one of our hero cars, seen from a dramatic top-down / high-oblique angle, already sliding into frame.
- Hard cut into **real gameplay** at almost the same angle/motion: car snapping through a fast corner or controlled handbrake drift.
- Very short Top Down RACE logo hit.

### On-screen text

**TOP DOWN RACE**

Optional subline:

**Drive it. Build it. Master it.**

This line is provisional.

### Voice-over option

> "This is Top Down RACE."

Nothing more yet.

---

## 0:05–0:20 — PROVE THE DRIVING

### Goal

Show immediately that the driving model is the centre of the game.

### Real gameplay shots

Use only strong captures:

- Fast corner entry with clean racing line.
- Handbrake drift with visible rear-light response.
- Tight overtake.
- Chicane taken diagonally and aggressively.
- Near-limit correction without spinning.
- Short burst of speed on a straight.

Avoid crashes unless one is visually spectacular and intentionally used as contrast.

### Editing

Cuts of roughly 2 seconds, preferably matched to beats.

Where possible, alternate camera zoom states so the dynamic camera system is visible naturally.

### On-screen text

One phrase only:

**BUILT AROUND THE DRIVE**

### Voice-over draft

> "We wanted a top-down racer where the car matters — braking, weight, grip, speed and the line you choose."

The final recording should sound conversational, not like an overproduced commercial.

---

## 0:20–0:34 — THE CARS HAVE PERSONALITY

### Goal

Make clear that cars are not cosmetic clones.

### Image

Fast garage sequence:

- Open Garage.
- Scroll through several visually distinct cars.
- Show one car card with the premium holographic treatment.
- Enter a car detail view.
- Quick return to racing with a different car.

Potential hero cars should include visually different silhouettes/brands rather than three similar vehicles.

### On-screen text

**15+ CARS. DIFFERENT CHARACTER.**

Number must be updated to the final release count before submission.

### Voice-over draft

> "Cars are built to feel different, not just look different."

If later telemetry or homologation results can be shown elegantly, one very brief stats overlay can reinforce this point.

---

## 0:34–0:49 — BUILD, EQUIP, IMPROVE

### Goal

Reveal that Top Down RACE has progression and systems beyond race selection.

### UI capture

Use fast, deliberate navigation through:

- Fabricación / workshop.
- Browse between cars with the arrows around the selected car name.
- Parts inventory.
- Equip or compare one component.
- Return to car overview showing resulting stats.

Do not linger on lists. The purpose is to communicate depth, not teach the whole system.

### On-screen text

**BUILD YOUR CAR**

Then, on the next beat:

**TUNE YOUR APPROACH**

### Voice-over draft

> "Build your garage, equip parts and decide what kind of car you want to take to the track."

---

## 0:49–1:05 — MORE THAN ONE WAY TO RACE

### Goal

Show breadth without turning the video into a menu tour.

### Image

Open the game-mode selector and move rapidly through the cards.

Highlight visually:

- Contrarreloj.
- Fantasma.
- Supervivencia.
- Duelo.
- Área de Pruebas.

Then cut into gameplay examples rather than leaving the cards on screen.

Suggested sequence:

- Ghost car / ghost mode visual.
- Survival pack of cars.
- Duel / close race moment.
- Área de Pruebas: asphalt → grass → off-road transition, then drift circle or gymkhana.

### On-screen text

**RACE. LEARN. EXPERIMENT.**

### Voice-over draft

> "Race for time, chase ghosts, survive traffic, duel rivals — or just experiment until the car does exactly what you want."

---

## 1:05–1:20 — CIRCUITS AND SURFACES

### Goal

Give the world variety and visual identity.

### Image

Montage of our actual circuits. Prioritise circuits that look visibly different from each other.

Potential sequence:

- Urban / Santa Cruz-style environment.
- Karting Tenerife.
- Another clean asphalt circuit.
- Raven Hollow only if performance and visuals are fully resolved before final capture.

Include at least one clear surface transition:

- Asphalt: no penalty.
- Grass: mild penalty.
- Off-road: strong penalty.

Raven Hollow should be framed as its own special surface character if included.

### Flow support

A 1-second generated transition may be used between environments, such as particles/dust crossing frame and revealing the next real circuit.

### On-screen text

**EVERY SURFACE CHANGES THE DRIVE**

### Voice-over draft

> "Different circuits and surfaces force you to change the way you drive."

---

## 1:20–1:34 — CONTROL IS PART OF THE GAME

### Goal

Show one of Top Down RACE's strongest mobile-specific arguments: the player is not forced into one control scheme.

### UI + gameplay capture

Very fast cuts showing:

- Palanca.
- Botones.
- Mando.
- Volante.
- Left-handed layout.
- Custom positioning/calibration screen.

Then cut instantly back to gameplay with a spectacular manoeuvre.

### On-screen text

**PLAY IT YOUR WAY**

### Voice-over draft

> "And because this is built for mobile, the controls adapt to the player — not the other way around."

This section should feel like proof of care and polish, not settings bureaucracy.

---

## 1:34–1:46 — THE HUMAN MOMENT / WHY IT EXISTS

### Goal

Give the judges something memorable beyond a feature checklist.

The project should briefly feel handmade, ambitious and personal without becoming sentimental or slowing the pace.

### Image

A rapid crescendo of the strongest real shots collected during development:

- Perfect apex.
- CPU battle/overtake once AI is presentation-ready.
- Handbrake drift.
- High-speed straight.
- Garage hero car.
- One striking card/skin.

A Flow-generated cinematic flash can bridge into the final shot, but finish on real gameplay or the real car/garage whenever possible.

### Voice-over draft

> "Top Down RACE started with a simple idea: make a mobile racing game we would genuinely want to keep playing. Then keep pushing every part of it until it felt right."

Alternative shorter version:

> "We built the racing game we wanted to keep playing — then kept refusing to leave the rough edges alone."

Final wording to be decided after a first rough edit.

---

## 1:46–1:55 — FINAL PUNCH

### Goal

Leave one clean memory: name + desire to try it.

### Image

Best possible 2–3 second gameplay shot, preferably:

- dramatic overtake into a corner;
- handbrake rotation followed by a clean exit;
- or extremely tight high-speed racing line.

Freeze or match-cut into logo.

### Final screen

**TOP DOWN RACE**

Provisional final claim:

**MASTER THE CAR. OWN THE LINE.**

Then a small Shipaton 2026 identifier if appropriate for the submission format.

### Voice-over

> "Top Down RACE."

Optional final line:

> "See you on the line."

Do not use both if it feels forced.

# Mandatory real-capture shot list

Before editing, collect multiple versions of each shot rather than trying to capture one perfect two-minute run.

### Racing

- Clean fast corner.
- Very tight apex.
- Handbrake drift, entry and exit.
- Brake lights under normal braking.
- Brake lights under handbrake.

- High-speed straight with stable steering.

### Garage / progression

- Scroll through car cards.

- Car detail.

### Controls

- Control-mode selector.
- Wheel control in use.
- Stick control in use.

### Circuits

Capture at least 3–4 visually distinct circuits, each with a short spectacular driving moment.

Do not capture Raven Hollow for the final video until it is confirmed performant on the target iPhone.

# Flow-generated clip plan

Flow should be used surgically. Initial target: **4–6 generated clips total**, most only 1–3 seconds in the final edit.

Candidate clips:

1. Opening hero-car drift / logo transition.
2. Close-up or top-down hero skin reveal.
3. Speed-energy transition between racing blocks.
4. Dust/surface transition into circuit montage.
5. Optional garage/car silhouette transition.
6. Final cinematic flash before the real closing gameplay shot.

Each prompt must be based on our actual car skins, circuit palette and UI branding whenever reference-image support is available.

Do not generate fake gameplay HUD, fake menus, fake game modes or imaginary mechanics.

---

# Audio strategy

The audio must make the game feel alive even if most of the footage is screen capture.

Final mix should contain:

- Music with clear build and identifiable beat for cuts.
- Real engine audio wherever it helps credibility.
- Tire scrub/drift sound accents.
- Short UI sounds during menu transitions.
- Low-impact whooshes only for cinematic transitions.
- Voice-over if it improves clarity; never allow VO to bury gameplay sound completely.

The first rough cut should be made both **with** and **without** voice-over. We should keep VO only if it adds information and personality faster than on-screen text alone.

---

# What the judges should know by 0:20

By the first twenty seconds, without reading a description, a judge should already understand:

- this is a real playable mobile racing game;
- the driving has more depth than casual lane-changing;
- the presentation has personality;
- there is enough polish to justify watching the rest.

If the rough cut does not accomplish this, the opening must be rebuilt regardless of how good later sections are.

---

# What the judges should know by the end

The finished video should leave these conclusions naturally:

**"This already feels like a real game."**

**"There is considerably more depth here than I expected."**

**"The developer clearly cares about how this feels on an actual phone."**

**"I want to download it and try a race."**

That last reaction is the real success metric for the submission video.

---

# Production rules

- Final duration must remain safely under the Shipaton video limit; target 1:45–1:55.
- Record gameplay on the actual target mobile device whenever possible.
- No debug/performance HUD in final captures unless a deliberate technical proof shot is later justified.
- No unstable or unfinished system appears in the final cut.
- Do not include Raven Hollow until its performance problem is confirmed solved on-device.
- Generated cinematic material must never imply functionality absent from the real game.
- Prefer several excellent 2–5 second captures over one long mediocre recording.
- Capture source video at the highest practical quality and crop only during final edit.
- Keep a clean version of every important shot without added titles so it can be reused.
